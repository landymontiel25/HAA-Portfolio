Background

In high school I played baseball, and I always felt like I was wasting time. I didn't know what else to do, so I kept playing. When I got to college, I wanted to stop and work on projects that interested me.

Before this year, I couldn't code. I knew how to use Claude and ChatGPT, but I didn't know how to turn them into products. Then I started building, and I learned something new almost every day. I learned how to merge code, open a pull request, fix a broken deployment, and use an API. I made 520 commits on Landmark Hunters in 29 days. I have read business books, but I learned more by building.

I failed first. My first project, BoatAficionado, started as a newsletter. I never marketed it, I waited too long to ship, and it never launched. The next project didn't work out either. Then I had nothing to build, and I hated it.

Landmark Hunters started after a trip to Europe with my family. My dad and I knew little about the places we visited and couldn't find anything that helped us learn. At first it was a fun project for trips. About 10 days in, my uncle and cousin tried it and saw no value.

A few days later, in philosophy class, I got out a paper and brainstormed. My friends and I kept going to the same bars, restaurants, and dorms, and I wanted to try new places. I turned the app into a planner that learns your taste and suggests better places. A week later I showed my uncle and cousin again, and they were much more interested. [one line from your cousin] If it didn't work for anyone else, it would work for me.

I fix causes, not symptoms. When duplicate landmarks let people farm points, I could have deleted them by hand. I built detection into Add a Landmark so duplicates get caught before they exist. When a feature stopped being useful, I rebuilt it in an hour. New users need about a week to build a taste profile, and most leave before then, so I built a simulator with synthetic users and ran 150 simulations to improve Mapr before real users showed up.

Why Me

I learn fast, and I changed how I work. With Landmark Hunters, it took over a month before anyone outside my house tried it, and it has 6 accounts today (still family). With VDate, I launched about 21 hours after my first commit, and it had 57 sign-ups in 5 days. I stopped waiting for a perfect product. I launch something simple and learn from real users.

I built VDate to learn what users want and how to get them to tell me. In both apps I added a Request a Feature button, because I won't have every idea, and users see things I don't. VDate is free. I get nothing from it. Students can send requests, but I have not shipped a change because of one yet. That is my next step.

I became an AI vampire. I would start coding at 10 pm and look up at 1 am, wondering where the time went. My favorite thing now is to go to my dorm, put on music, and work on VDate and Landmark Hunters. I work on this because I find it interesting. If these apps don't work, I can use what I learned on new projects with new people.

Why I Need Horowitz Andreesen Academy

What I need is help with my hardest problem. I can explain Landmark Hunters well, but if a stranger doesn't see the value in 10 seconds, they leave. I don't know yet how to fix that. The Academy has people who have done it, and I want to learn from them and build with them.
